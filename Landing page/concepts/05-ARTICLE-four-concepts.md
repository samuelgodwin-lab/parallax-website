## Four cities, four broken products, and what we'd build instead

*No client asked for these. We picked the problem in each of our markets that costs people the most, and designed what we'd build if someone handed us the brief tomorrow.*

**In one paragraph.** Parallax works in Dubai, Munich, Denver and Northeast India. For each city we chose the sector where the digital experience fails people worst, and designed a full product concept for it. In Dubai, an app that follows an off-plan buyer from launch day to keys. In Munich, an insurance flow people finish, accessible by law. In Denver, a control room that ranks alarms by consequence. In Northeast India, an admissions form that survives a weak signal. Each is labelled a concept, has no client, and states what we'd measure rather than claiming results.

### Why design for a client you don't have?

Because a portfolio only shows the problems clients brought us. A concept shows the problem we'd choose, and how we'd think it through before anyone pays for it.

We picked each sector by two tests: where the problem is most severe for the person using the product, and where fixing it is worth the most to the business. The figures come from the research we did for each market page. The outcomes are targets we'd design to, not numbers we've achieved.

### Dubai: what should an off-plan buyer app do?

Most of Dubai's 270,000 property sales last year were off-plan: a home bought before it exists, then waited on for three years. The buyer's whole experience is a launch site, a broker's WhatsApp and a payment plan in a PDF, and many buyers are abroad for all of it.

The concept, *Sold before it's built*, designs one product for the buyer, with the broker and the developer working from the same data:

- **Live inventory** instead of a floor-plan PDF that's out of date by lunchtime: pick the floor, the view, the unit.
- **One payment schedule** for every instalment, receipt and escrow payment, instead of email reminders.
- **Monthly site updates** against the handover date, instead of progress by rumour.

What we'd measure: under 5 minutes from launch to reservation, zero payment calls, 12 site updates a year, every receipt in the app. → [See the Dubai concept](/work/concept-off-plan-dubai)

### Munich: why do customers abandon insurance forms?

Insurers in Munich compete on price and cover, then lose the customer at step three of a form. Forty fields on one page, jargon nobody explains, and a document upload that fails on a phone. Since June 2025 Germany's BFSG also requires these flows to be accessible, with fines of up to €100,000. A form that loses customers is now also a form that breaks the law.

The concept, *Insurance people finish*, redesigns a home-contents policy from quote to claim:

- **One question per screen**, with answers filled in where we already know them.
- **The policy as a page** you can read, search and share, not a PDF.
- **Claims filed from a phone** in minutes, photos included, instead of by phone and post.

What we'd measure: under 4 minutes from quote to policy, WCAG 2.2 AA on every screen, zero BFSG blockers at launch. → [See the Munich concept](/work/concept-insurance-munich)

### Denver: what makes a control-room dashboard usable?

Colorado's energy operators run world-class hardware from software that was specified, never designed. A storm front puts hundreds of alarms on screen in the same red. Answering "which site is losing output?" takes four tools and a spreadsheet. At shift change, what the last operator knew leaves with them.

The concept, *Tools that match the hardware*, designs the operations view for the person on shift, dark mode first:

- **Alarms ranked by consequence**, with the critical three on top.
- **One view of the whole fleet**, drilling down to a single asset.
- **Handover built in**: what changed, what's open, who owns it.

What we'd measure: the 3 critical alarms always on top, fleet to asset on one screen, a shift handover in under 5 minutes, no handover spreadsheets. → [See the Denver concept](/work/concept-energy-ops-denver)

### Northeast India: how do you design admissions for a weak signal?

A student in the hills applies on a phone, on a signal that drops, and the form loses everything when it does. The upload fails, the fee payment opens on a different site, and the only way to check status is to travel to the office. The portal was built on a desktop, on office broadband.

The concept, *Admissions students don't fight*, designs for the phone the student owns and the signal they actually have:

- **Every answer saved on the phone**, sent when the signal returns.
- **Documents photographed and compressed** on the device before upload.
- **Status by SMS** at every stage, in English, Khasi, Assamese and Hindi.

What we'd measure: zero lost applications, a full application in under 15 minutes, working on 2G. → [See the Northeast India concept](/work/concept-admissions-northeast)

### What do the four have in common?

The expensive failure is never on the homepage. It's the moment the person is alone with the software, in the worst conditions they'll meet: a buyer in another time zone, a customer at step three, an operator at 3 a.m. during a storm, a student on one bar of signal.

So all four start from that moment, not from the happy path. All four are designed on one system, so each screen follows the same rules. And all four are accessible from the first screen, because in each of these markets that is either the law or about to be. → [The accessibility deadlines in all four markets](/articles/accessibility-deadlines-2026)

### Questions people ask

**What is a design concept?**
A complete product proposal for a real problem, made without a client: the research, the journey, the key screens and the targets it would be measured against. Unlike a case study, it shows how a studio thinks rather than what a client approved.

**Are these real clients?**
No. Each is labelled a concept and carries no brand. The problems, sectors and figures are real, taken from our research on each market.

**Are the outcomes results?**
No. They are design targets, listed under "What we'd measure". A concept can't have results until it ships with real users.

**What does each concept include?**
A mapped journey, the key desktop and mobile screens built on our design system, the roles involved, and four measurable targets. Each has its own page under Work.

**Can a concept become a real project?**
Yes. The flows and screens are a starting point that can be tested with your users and adapted to your brand, data and systems, which shortens the start of a project.

**Why these four sectors?**
Each is where the gap between what the business sells and what the software delivers is widest in that city: off-plan property in Dubai, insurance in Munich, energy operations in Colorado, and university admissions in Northeast India.
