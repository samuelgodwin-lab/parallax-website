# Concepts — copy for sign-off

Four unbranded concept projects, one per market, each on the sector where the
problem is most severe or worth the most. Every concept lives in two places:

- **A — Market page section.** A new, separate section on the market page
  (after the sectors bento, before the compliance band). It's a short teaser:
  label, heading, lede (≤ 60 words), problem → outcome pairs, link.
- **B — Concept page.** A full page in the YVT/BMW format (`case_studies` row,
  server-rendered). Also listed under its own **Concepts** group on /work,
  separate from client projects.

Rules across all four:
- "Concept" in the label, the client field and the Work card. No brand names.
- Outcomes are **design targets**: four per concept, a figure plus a two-to-three-word label
  (the YVT format), under "What we'd *measure.*" — never presented as results.
- **No quote block.** A quote with no client behind it would be a fabricated
  testimonial.
- Figures come from the market research briefs only.
- `*word*` = accent italic, as on the rest of the site.

## 1 · Dubai — Off-plan real estate

### A — Section on /dubai/

**Label:** Concept — Off-plan real estate
**H2:** From launch to keys, *in one app.*
**Lede:** Most of Dubai's 270,000 sales last year were off-plan: bought from a
launch site, a broker's phone and a payment plan in a PDF, then followed for
three years by email. We designed what the buyer should have instead.

**Problem → outcome**
| Today | With the concept |
|---|---|
| Units chosen from a PDF floor plan | Live inventory: pick the floor, the view, the unit |
| Payment milestones in email reminders | Every instalment, receipt and escrow payment in one schedule |
| Construction progress by rumour | Monthly site updates against the handover date |

**overview_heading:** Three years between *signing and keys.*
**overview:** An off-plan buyer commits to a home that doesn't exist yet, and

**challenge_body:** Units are chosen from a floor-plan PDF that is out of date

at all. Many buyers are abroad, so all of it happens at a distance.

## 2 · Munich — Insurance onboarding

### A — Section on /munich/

**Label:** Concept — Insurance
**H2:** An insurance flow people *finish.*
**Lede:** Munich is Europe's insurance capital, and its products are still sold
through forms most customers abandon. Since June 2025 those forms must also be
accessible by law. We designed an onboarding and claims flow that does both.

**Problem → outcome**
| Today | With the concept |
|---|---|
| Forty fields on one page | One question at a time, with answers filled in for you |
| A policy delivered as a PDF | The policy as a page you can read, search and share |
| Claims by phone and post | A claim filed from your phone in minutes, photos included |

**Link:** See the concept →

### B — Concept page

| Field | Copy |
|---|---|
| client | Concept |
| title | Insurance people finish |
| industry | Insurance |
| services | Product Design · Interface Design · Accessibility |
| year | 2026 |
| slug | `concept-insurance-munich` |
| meta title | Accessible Insurance Onboarding Concept, Munich — Parallax |
| meta description | A concept for insurance onboarding and claims in Munich: a flow customers finish, built to WCAG 2.2 AA for the BFSG. |

**overview_heading:** The policy is fine. *The form is the problem.*
**overview:** Insurers in Munich compete on price and cover. Then they lose the
customer at step three of a form. This concept redesigns a home-contents policy
from quote to claim: it asks less, explains more, and works with a screen
reader from the first field.

**challenge_heading:** Abandoned forms, and now *a legal deadline.*
**challenge_body:** Long single-page forms, jargon nobody explains, and a
document upload that fails on a phone. Since June 2025 the BFSG requires these
flows to be accessible, with fines of up to €100,000 and warning letters from
competitors (Abmahnungen). A form that loses customers is now also a form that
breaks the law.

**heading_stats:** The flow, *mapped.*
**stats_description:** A home-contents policy from first quote to settled
claim, designed for the customer and checked against WCAG 2.2 AA at every step.
**stats:**
- 2 — Journeys (onboarding, claims)
- 5 — Steps to a policy
- 4 — Key screens
- 2 — Languages (German, English)

**heading_outcomes:** What we'd *measure.*
**outcomes** (design targets: figure + two-to-three-word label, as YVT):
- **<4 min** — Quote to policy
- **1** — Question per screen
- **AA** — WCAG 2.2, every screen
- **0** — BFSG blockers at launch

**heading_digital:** Quote to claim, *on one phone.*

## 3 · Denver — Energy operations

### A — Section on /denver/

**Label:** Concept — Aerospace & energy
**H2:** A control room that reads *at a glance.*
**Lede:** Colorado's energy and aerospace operators run billions of dollars of
hardware from software engineers built for themselves. Four tools are open at
once, alarms arrive in floods, and the shift handover happens in a spreadsheet.
We designed the operations view an operator would actually choose.

**Problem → outcome**
| Today | With the concept |
|---|---|
| Hundreds of alarms, all the same red | Alarms ranked by consequence, the critical three on top |
| Four tools open to answer one question | One view of the whole fleet, drill-down to a single asset |
| Shift handover in a spreadsheet | Handover built in: what changed, what's open, who owns it |

**Link:** See the concept →

### B — Concept page

| meta title | Energy Operations Dashboard Concept, Denver — Parallax |
| meta description | A concept for a renewable fleet's control room: alarms ranked by consequence, one view of every asset, and shift handover built in. |

**overview_heading:** Hardware that's *engineered.* Software that *isn't.*
**overview:** A renewable fleet runs wind and solar sites across a state, and
it is monitored by software that was specified, never designed. This concept
redesigns the operations view for the person on shift: what is wrong, what
matters most, and what to do next.

**challenge_heading:** When everything is urgent, *nothing is.*
**challenge_body:** A storm front puts four hundred alarms on screen, all in
the same red. Answering "which site is losing output?" takes four tools and a
spreadsheet. At shift change, what the last operator knew leaves with them.
The hardware is world-class. The screen in front of it isn't.

**heading_stats:** The control room, *mapped.*
**stats_description:** One fleet view, one asset view, one handover, all built
on a design system the operator's own team can extend.
**stats:**
- 3 — Core views (fleet, asset, handover)
- 1 — Design system, handed over
- 4 — Key screens
- 24/7 — Night shift first (dark mode)

**heading_outcomes:** What we'd *measure.*
**outcomes** (design targets: figure + two-to-three-word label, as YVT):
- **3** — Critical alarms on top
- **1** — Screen, fleet to asset
- **<5 min** — Shift handover
- **0** — Handover spreadsheets

**heading_digital:** The whole fleet, *on one screen.*

## 4 · Northeast India — Admissions

### A — Section on /northeast-india/

**Label:** Concept — Education
**H2:** Admissions on *a weak signal.*
**Lede:** A student in the hills applies on a phone, on a signal that drops,
and the form loses everything when it does. Then they travel to the office to
ask what happened. We designed an admissions flow for ten thousand applicants
that saves every step and answers its own questions.

**Problem → outcome**
| Today | With the concept |
|---|---|
| A form that times out and loses everything | Every answer saved on the phone, sent when the signal returns |
| Uploads that fail on a weak signal | Documents photographed and compressed on the phone |
| "Come to the office to check" | Status by SMS at every stage, from submitted to admitted |

**Link:** See the concept →

### B — Concept page

| Field | Copy |
|---|---|
| client | Concept |
| title | Admissions students don't fight |
| industry | Education |
| services | Product Design · Interface Design · Accessibility |
| year | 2026 |
| slug | `concept-admissions-northeast` |
| meta title | Mobile Admissions Portal Concept, Northeast India — Parallax |
| meta description | A concept for university admissions in Northeast India: a phone-first flow that survives a weak signal, saves every step and reports status by SMS. |

**overview_heading:** Ten thousand applications, *one helpdesk.*
**overview:** Admissions is the highest-volume, highest-friction software in
the region, and the most important form a student fills in all year. This
concept designs it for the phone the student owns and the signal they actually
have, with the admissions office working from the same live view.

**challenge_heading:** The form fails, *so the student travels.*
**challenge_body:** The portal was built on a desktop, on office broadband. On
a phone in the hills, the form times out, the upload fails and the fee payment
opens in a different site. With no status page, the only way to check is a
queue at the office. The applicants are lost, and the helpdesk fills up.

**heading_stats:** The admission, *mapped.*
**stats_description:** One applicant journey and one office view, designed
for low bandwidth first and checked against India's accessibility standard
(IS 17802) throughout.
**stats:**
- 2 — Sides designed (applicant, admissions office)
- 5 — Stages to admission
- 4 — Key screens
- 4 — SMS languages (English, Khasi, Assamese, Hindi)

**heading_outcomes:** What we'd *measure.*
**outcomes** (design targets: figure + two-to-three-word label, as YVT):
- **0** — Lost applications
- **<15 min** — Full application
- **2G** — Works on it
- **5** — Status texts, by SMS

**heading_digital:** The whole admission, *on a phone.*
